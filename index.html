02 / Projects

Treasury Yields and Stock Valuations After a Fed Balance Sheet Shock

Internship project · March to August 2025

  • Python
  • pandas
  • statsmodels
  • Matplotlib
  • Objective: 59 quarters of U.S. data (2007 Q2 to 2021 Q4) were analyzed to estimate how the 10-year Treasury yield and the S&P 500 price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio responded to an unexpected expansion of the Fed’s balance sheet.
  • Model selection: A vector error correction model (VECM) was used because the data suggested a long-run relationship among the series. Because that evidence depended on the lag length, the results were compared with those from a vector autoregression (VAR) in quarterly changes.
  • Findings: Following a one-standard-deviation balance sheet shock (about a 9% increase), the Treasury yield response was estimated to level off at −0.20 percentage points. A negative P/E response was estimated, but its 90% bootstrap confidence band included zero at most horizons.
  • Limitation: Causality could not be established because the balance sheet expansion could not be fully separated from the market stress that may have prompted it.
